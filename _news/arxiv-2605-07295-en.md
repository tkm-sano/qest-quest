## A paper on a distributed switching protocol for quantum networks has been published on arXiv.

**Paper title: A Distributed Switching Protocol for Quantum Networks**

Efficient use of network resources is a critical challenge for scalable entanglement-based quantum networks. Recent architectures using memoryless optical switches have attracted attention as a way to forward entangled photons and share costly resources, such as high-efficiency Bell State Analyzers (BSAs), across a network. However, switching also makes multiplexing and resource allocation more complex than in individual-link settings.

This work proposes a distributed switching protocol for unbuffered, multidrop quantum networks in a photonic synchronization domain. The protocol establishes a link between two end nodes using a shared BSA in the switched network. The two end nodes cooperatively choose the target BSA node with the lowest path cost, then independently reserve each path. Using bi-path reservations, the protocol allocates resources in a distributed manner. Simulations on Q-Fly network topologies show high link-establishment success and stable performance even as network load increases.

- Authors

  Aman Yacob Tekleab, Yifeng Shen, Yoshii Yutaro, Amin Taherkhani, Rodney Van Meter, Shota Nagayama
